Getting started

Download HOPE Labs, sign in to Grace once, and let it open its windows.

What you need

  • A Grace account at Texas A&M HPRC, its password, and Duo on your phone.
  • A browser on the same computer. HOPE Labs opens its page in your default browser; the instructions here are for Chrome.
  • Access to the tools. HOPE Labs opens the lab's own copies under /scratch/group/sflab, which only members of the lab's group on Grace can reach. Anyone else installs the tools in their own scratch first: Your own copies on Grace.

Download and start it

ComputerDownloadThe first time
WindowsHOPE-Labs-windows.zipUnzip the whole folder, not only the program, and run HOPE-Labs.exe inside it. It is not signed, so Windows says Windows protected your PC: choose More info, then Run anyway.
macOS, Apple siliconHOPE-Labs-mac-apple-silicon.zipUnzip it, then right click the app and choose Open. After the first time it opens like any other app.
Any computer with Python 3.10 or newerthe sourceThe two lines below, in the checkout. Python needs tkinter, which most installs include.

Both downloads are on the releases page of the HOPE Labs repository. From the source:

pip install paramiko
python -m hope_labs

Signing in

HOPE Labs opens a small window first. It is the sign in, and it stays open while you work: closing it signs you out.

BoxWhat goes in it
ClusterGrace (TAMU). ACES (TAMU) and Other (SLURM) are there too; ACES signs in with a certificate rather than a password, and not every tool is installed there.
AddressFilled in when you pick the cluster: grace.hprc.tamu.edu.
NetIDYour NetID alone, such as jdoe. If you type your TAMU email address, the part before the @ is used, and the log says so.

Press Sign in and open HOPE Labs. Grace asks for your password, then Duo asks how you want to approve. Each question arrives in a small window of its own, with the text exactly as Grace sent it; answer it as you would in a terminal. When you have approved, the panel called What is happening says Signed in on and the login node's name, then HOPE Labs is open, and the page opens in your browser by itself.

One Duo approval covers every tool. HOPE Labs holds the one connection for as long as you are signed in, and every tool you open goes through it.

If the page does not open, Open the page in the same window opens it again, and the log shows its address to paste into a browser. The address carries a token that only this session knows, so keep it to yourself; it changes every time you sign in.

The boxes are remembered for next time, in a file called .hope_labs.json in your home folder on your own computer. Your password is never kept. The same file can point HOPE Labs at copies of the tools in your own scratch.

Allow its windows

Each tool opens in a browser window of its own, which Chrome treats as a pop-up. The first time, Chrome blocks it, and HOPE Labs says so in a message at the bottom right of the page. Click Pop-up blocked at the right of the address bar, choose Always allow, and pick the tool again.

Chrome remembers that choice for the page's address, and the address includes its port. HOPE Labs asks for the same port every time you sign in, worked out from your account name, so the choice holds from one session to the next. If that port is busy, the log says which port the page is on instead, and Chrome may ask once more.

The page that opens is the Tools page. Pick a tool and press Launch.
